# Voice

DRAFT — carried over from a sister site and adapted for gutters. Review and correct before it's used to write blog posts or service copy.

## Who's talking

A working gutter crew's owner/operator, not a marketing department. Someone who's stood on the ladder. Confident about the trade, plain about the business side (pricing, scheduling, what's included).

## Sentence rhythm

- Lead with the claim, then the reason. "Most gutter failures start at the hangers. Spacing them too far apart lets a run sag under a full load of rain."
- Short declaratives mixed with one longer sentence per paragraph — not a wall of short choppy fragments, not run-ons either.
- Cut the sentence when the point lands. Don't add a second clause that just restates it.
- Paragraphs of 2-4 sentences. One idea each.

## Vocabulary

- Concrete nouns over abstract ones: "hangers," "fascia," "pitch," "outlet," "written estimate" — not "quality," "value," "excellence."
- Trade specifics are a feature, not jargon to hide: gauge, K-style, hidden hangers, downspout outlets, pitch per ten feet.
- Numbers instead of adjectives wherever possible. Not "fast turnaround" — "one to two days."
- Plain contractions (it's, don't, we'll) — conversational, not stiff.
- No superlatives without a number backing them up ("best," "top-rated" — only if a specific ranking/rating is cited).
- "Seamless" is the product name for gutters formed from a coil. Use it as a product term only, never as a marketing adjective.

## Formatting

- Headlines are short, declarative, often end with a period even mid-sentence style: "Built On the Hangers, Not Shortcuts."
- One eyebrow label above section headings (small caps style), stating the section's topic plainly: "Why It Holds Up," "Questions Answered."
- Stats presented as bare numbers with a one-word label underneath, not sentences.
- Bullets for parallel lists of features/steps; prose for anything that needs reasoning or a tradeoff explained.

## Anti-patterns — never do this

- No exclamation marks.
- No emojis.
- No AI-tell phrases: "unlock," "leverage," "world-class," "elevate," "in today's fast-paced world," "look no further," "whether you're X or Y," "at the end of the day," "when it comes to."
- No rhetorical questions used as filler openers ("Ever wonder why...?").
- No vague reassurance without a mechanism behind it. Don't say "quality you can trust" — say what specifically makes it hold up (hanger spacing, gauge, pitch, warranty terms).
- Don't hide the boundary. If a job isn't a fit (lowest-bid shoppers, gutters that just need cleaning, a wet foundation that needs a longer extension instead of new gutters), say so directly — this is the site's single biggest trust signal and it's used consistently in the `reasonsParagraph` copy across service pages.

## Tells that it's AI-written — check for these before shipping

- Opening a post or section with a broad, ungrounded statement ("Gutters are one of the most important parts of your home") instead of the direct answer.
- Symmetrical list structures where every bullet has the exact same sentence shape and length.
- Hedging language: "can help," "may vary," "it's important to note that."
- Conclusion paragraphs that just summarize what was already said ("In conclusion, gutters...").
- Overuse of "not just X, but Y" or "isn't just about X — it's about Y" constructions.
- Three-item lists used reflexively even when the real answer has two items or four.
- Transition words doing no work: "Furthermore," "Moreover," "Additionally" — the existing copy just starts the next sentence.
- Every paragraph the same length. Real writing varies — a one-sentence paragraph is fine.
